{# Amps and the contacts, and nothing derived from voltage. The Shelly's own supply is a 120 V outlet, not a tap off the phase the clamps are around, so its voltage reading belongs to a different circuit. Current does not care: a CT measures the field around a conductor and is independent of what the meter is using as a voltage reference. Watts and power factor very much do care, because real power needs voltage and current from the same phase with the right angle between them, and there is no reason to think that angle is right here. So they are not shown. Two sections, and the split is what each answers. The panel above is what is happening now: lamps, the screen, live amps, when a pump last started. These cards are how it has been running, which moves over weeks and is where a failing pump shows up first. Typical load is the median of every reading above the running threshold over the last week, which ignores the starting surge without needing a rule about what counts as one. Under it, how that compares with the four weeks before, because one number is not information: sixteen amps is fine or alarming entirely depending on what it was last month. The run contact and the overload used to sit here too, and the disagreement between the contact and the clamp was the useful part: a closed contactor drawing nothing is a motor that is not turning. Putting them back needs a way to say which input carries pump 1's run contact, and inputs are free text labels now. See NOTES.md. #}
