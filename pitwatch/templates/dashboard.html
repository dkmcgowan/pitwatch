{# Green while it is running. The current below says the same thing in a number, which is what a screen reader gets; this is for the glance from the doorway. #} i What these numbers mean

Current is what the clamp is reading right now, which on a pit that runs for seconds at a time is usually zero.

Last run and runs today come from the current rising off nothing. The average beside the count is an ordinary day over the last month, so today has something to be read against.

Typical load is the middle reading of every one taken while the pump was running this week, which leaves out the starting surge without needing a rule about what counts as one. It is the number to watch: a pump drawing more than it did a month ago is a pump on its way to a problem.

FLA is the full load amps printed on the motor plate. Nothing is calculated from it. It is there so the typical load has something real to sit against, and a pump drawing more than its own rating is worth knowing about.

{# Amps and the contacts, and nothing derived from voltage. The Shelly's own supply is a 120 V outlet, not a tap off the phase the clamps are around, so its voltage reading belongs to a different circuit. Current does not care: a CT measures the field around a conductor and is independent of what the meter is using as a voltage reference. Watts and power factor very much do care, because real power needs voltage and current from the same phase with the right angle between them, and there is no reason to think that angle is right here. So they are not shown. Two sections, and the split is what each answers. The panel above is the panel: what the controller and the floats are doing. These cards are the pumps themselves, from the clamps, which is both what one is drawing now and how it has been running. Typical load is the median of every reading above the running threshold over the last week, which ignores the starting surge without needing a rule about what counts as one. Under it, how that compares with the four weeks before, because one number is not information: sixteen amps is fine or alarming entirely depending on what it was last month. The run contact and the overload used to sit here too, and the disagreement between the contact and the clamp was the useful part: a closed contactor drawing nothing is a motor that is not turning. Putting them back needs a way to say which input carries pump 1's run contact, and inputs are free text labels now. See NOTES.md. #}
