Calls for water

A call is one filling of the pit: the floats rose, one or both pumps ran, the pit emptied. Two pumps answering one call is still one call, which is why runs sits higher than calls by exactly the number that took both. A call that took both, or reached the high float, is marked on top of its bar.

Rain hangs from the top on its own scale, so the two are not read against each other. The thing worth asking about is a tall call bar under a dry sky. Groundwater lags: a wet Tuesday shows up again on Wednesday.

Run length

One dot per run, from the panel closing the run contact to opening it. The dashed line is the middle of the window for that pump.

A pump taking twenty seconds where it used to take twelve is moving less water per second: a worn impeller, a partly blocked line, or a check valve letting water back down. It shows here before it shows in the current, and it works on a pump with no clamp.

No clamp has read current yet. The current transformers are per pump, and a channel without one reads zero.

Current draw

One dot per run: the steady current while the contact was closed, with the starting surge dropped. Every motor pulls several times its running current for the moment it starts, which says nothing about this pump. The dashed line is the middle of the window.

What is left is the part that moves when something is wrong: a motor working against a blockage climbs. A pump whose clamp has never read current is left out rather than drawn along the floor.

Time of day

Every call in the window stacked into one day, by the hour, in this building's own time. Four high on the 30 day window is four calls in that hour across the month, not four a day.

It says where the water comes from. Even around the clock is groundwater and follows the weather; quiet at three in the morning and busy at midday is the building's, and follows the people in it.
