Calls for water

A call is one filling of the pit: the floats rose, one or both pumps ran, the pit emptied. Two pumps answering one call is one call, which is why the count of calls above is not the count of runs beside it. Runs sits higher by exactly the number of calls that took both.

Counted from the panel's run contacts, so it is exact rather than a floor. A call that took both pumps, or that reached the high float, is marked on top of its bar.

This is the number that moves when it rains.

Run length

One dot per run, from the moment the panel closed the run contact to the moment it opened it. The dashed line is the middle of the window for that pump.

A pump that starts taking twenty seconds where it used to take twelve is moving less water per second: a worn impeller, a partly blocked line, or a check valve letting water back down. It shows here before it shows in the current.

This works on a pump with no clamp fitted, which is the point.

{# What the motor drew while it ran. Only for a clamp that has proved it can see current: a channel with no CT reads a convincing zero. #}

Current draw

No clamp has read current yet. The current transformers are per pump, and a channel without one reads zero.

Current draw

One dot per run: the steady current, which is the median of the readings taken while the contact was closed with the first one dropped. The dashed line is the middle of the window for that pump.

The starting surge is not drawn. Every motor pulls several times its running current for the moment it starts, so a chart of that is a chart of a fact about induction motors rather than about this pump, and being the larger number it was the one the eye read. What is left is the part that moves when something is wrong: a motor working against a blockage climbs, and one that has lost a phase or is losing its bearings climbs further.

A pump whose clamp has never read current is left out rather than drawn along the floor. Zero from a channel with no CT on it is not a measurement.

Time of day

Every call in the window stacked into a single day, by the hour, in this building's own time. A bar four high on the 30 day window means four calls in that hour across the whole month, not four a day.

It says where the water is coming from, which is the one thing nothing else on this page can tell you. A pit that runs evenly around the clock is taking groundwater, and it will follow the weather. One that goes quiet at three in the morning and doubles at midday is taking the building's, and it will follow the people in it.

Which pump answered is not shown. The panel alternates, so it is half and half in every hour and says nothing about the water.
